Cataract Surgery How do I know if I need cataract surgery?

Cataracts cause progressive blurring, glare, and difficulty seeing at night. If these symptoms interfere with daily activities like reading, driving, or recognizing faces, surgery may be recommended. Our clinic uses femtosecond laser-assisted cataract surgery for precision and faster recovery. A comprehensive exam will determine your candidacy.

Cataract Surgery What is the recovery time after cataract surgery?

Most patients return to normal activities within 24–48 hours. Full visual stabilization typically occurs over 2–4 weeks. We provide detailed post-op instructions and schedule follow-ups at our Thessaloniki clinic to monitor healing. Avoid heavy lifting and swimming for two weeks.

LASIK & Vision Correction Am I a good candidate for LASIK or femtosecond laser correction?

Ideal candidates are over 21, have stable vision for at least one year, and have no active eye diseases. Our advanced diagnostic mapping (including corneal topography and wavefront analysis) ensures personalized treatment. We offer both LASIK and PRK options. Schedule a free consultation to learn more.

LASIK & Vision Correction Is the procedure painful? How long does it take?

Numbing drops eliminate discomfort during the procedure. Each eye takes about 10–15 minutes. You may feel slight pressure but no pain. Most patients describe the experience as quick and comfortable. Our team will guide you every step of the way.

Retina & Macula What are the early signs of macular degeneration?

Early signs include blurred central vision, difficulty reading small print, and distorted straight lines (metamorphopsia). Regular dilated eye exams are crucial, especially after age 50. Our clinic offers OCT imaging and anti-VEGF injections for advanced care.

Retina & Macula Can diabetic retinopathy be reversed?

While damage cannot be fully reversed, early detection and treatment can prevent vision loss and improve outcomes. Our retina specialists use laser therapy, intravitreal injections, and advanced monitoring. Strict blood sugar control is essential. We partner with your endocrinologist for comprehensive care.

Glaucoma What are the first symptoms of glaucoma?

Open-angle glaucoma often has no early symptoms — that's why it's called the "silent thief of sight." Regular eye pressure checks and optic nerve exams are vital. Acute angle-closure glaucoma causes sudden eye pain, headache, and blurred vision — seek emergency care immediately.

Glaucoma Is glaucoma treatment lifelong?

Yes, glaucoma is a chronic condition that requires ongoing management. Treatment includes prescription eye drops, laser procedures (SLT), or surgery (trabeculectomy). Our team creates a personalized plan to maintain your vision and quality of life. Regular follow-ups are essential.

Pediatric Ophthalmology When should my child have their first eye exam?

The first comprehensive eye exam should be at 6 months of age, then again at age 3, and before starting school. Early detection of amblyopia (lazy eye), strabismus (crossed eyes), or refractive errors is critical. Our pediatric specialists use child-friendly techniques for accurate, comfortable exams.

Pediatric Ophthalmology Can screen time damage my child's eyes?

Excessive screen time can cause digital eye strain, dry eyes, and may contribute to myopia progression. We recommend the 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds), proper lighting, and regular outdoor activity. Schedule an exam if you notice squinting or headaches.

General Eye Care How often should I have a comprehensive eye exam?

Adults under 40 with no risk factors should have an exam every 2 years. After 40, annually is recommended. Those with diabetes, family history of glaucoma, or other conditions may need more frequent visits. Our clinic offers state-of-the-art diagnostic technology for thorough evaluations.
